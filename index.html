03 / Customer Research

Validate the idea in the real world.

A three-stage research sequence: concept reaction, assisted demonstration, and real play.

01Concept test

Private website & survey

Invite golfers to review the Why/Rules content, watch a sample round, and complete an initial survey.

Signals to collect

Format interest · perceived complexity · group fit · willingness to try and pay

02Demonstration

Simulator / practice test

Have participants experience shot-making and gameplay in a controlled session, then complete a follow-up survey.

Signals to collect

Rules comprehension · shot engagement · enjoyment · objections

032027 pilot

Real course round

Observe the full game under genuine playing conditions during the spring/summer pilot.

Signals to collect

Completion · replay intention · paid conversion · group adoption

Research guardrails

  • Recruit golfers aged 18 and older for Stage 1.
  • Use a private-preview confidentiality confirmation.
  • Track who watched the sample round.
  • Capture concerns, including group reluctance to play.
  • Keep concept interest distinct from confirmed adoption.

04 / Competition

Compete for the experience, not the feature list.

Six/Six competes with other playing formats, familiar golf games, and tools competing for attention—not just other apps.

Alternative formats

Different ways to play

Speedgolf, FlingGolf, and sixes-style formats change rules or play patterns.

Golf games

Different ways to score

Scramble, skins, Wolf, Nassau, and Stableford are familiar alternatives for social play.

Digital tools

Different ways to track

18Birdies, Garmin Golf, and Arccos help track rounds, yardages and performance.

Six/Six differentiator

Fewer clubs. More imagination.

The equipment constraint changes shot selection and decision-making while keeping golfers on familiar courses. Its most important hypothesis is that players will enjoy it enough to play again.

Competitive comparisons are directional, not a current feature-by-feature market audit.
